### Static equilibrium with the passive orbit (M5, Task 5.2)

The FEM no longer uses the table forces as they are, nor M4's zero-moment balance: the six
forces are changed by the smallest weighted amount that cancels the passive orbital moment
`−Kθ` of `data/eom/passive-orbit.json` (about 0.118 N·mm per degree horizontally, Collins 1981
by excerpt), each between zero and its capacity, without M4's constraint that the total tension
stay fixed (`eyebiomech/forces/equilibrium.py` explains why that constraint unloaded the
agonist). Every gaze of the ±30° lattice then keeps every muscle the table loads. Forces in N
(lateral, medial, superior, inferior rectus, superior, inferior oblique):

| Gaze | Passive moment (N·mm, globe frame) | Table (length proxy) | Equilibrium |
|---|---|---|---|
| primary (0, 0) | 0 | 0.116, 0.147, 0.168, 0.085, 0.116, 0.116 | 0.160, 0.076, 0.100, 0.092, 0.106, 0.109 |
| near (−5.47, −19.92) | (2.347, −0.618, 0) | 0.075, 0.199, 0, 0.192, 0.269, 0 | 0.086, 0.095, 0, 0.140, 0.088, 0.001 |
| 30° abduction | (0, 3.53, 0) | 0.367, 0, 0.171, 0.083, 0.149, 0.083 | 0.281, 0.001, 0.096, 0.100, 0.096, 0.098 |
| 30° adduction | (0, −3.53, 0) | 0, 0.464, 0.157, 0.091, 0.080, 0.152 | 0.002, 0.141, 0.112, 0.095, 0.089, 0.102 |

The change is large even in primary gaze (lateral rectus +38%, medial −48%, superior −40%): the
length proxy with the tabled capacities is far from torque balance, and the proxy's agonist
moment at 30° is about twice the passive moment. The muscle load magnitudes are therefore weakly
constrained by this model, which matters for any conclusion about the size of muscle-induced
astigmatism (M4 Deviation 15 found a fivefold sensitivity to the balancing choice).

Forces are `activation × maxForceN` with the length-proxy activation (an approximation, spec
§4.4 v1) and the force capacities of `data/eom/forces.json` (partly assumed). At the lattice
corners the antagonists relax to zero force.

(the clamped node stays put by construction; its shift is kept as `retinaClampMm`), writes the
heat-map field relative to the lens, and fits the scleral height change.

normalized to 4 mm): c(2,−2) −0.200, c(2,0) +0.946, c(2,2) −1.060. Shifts: anterior apex
+62.3 µm, lens +63.4 µm, retina −0.2 µm (clamp node +0.1 µm), so the cornea and lens move

The induced astigmatism is not mesh-converged: ΔJ0 moves by −16% and then +29% between the

exactly, a locally refined cornea) is a follow-up.

## The response library (M5, Task 5.8)

Built at `n_face = 20`, one layer (the mesh study above), in 10 681 s on the cloud session's 4
cores shared with other jobs (about 45 min per stiffness level). The build ran from the working
tree whose export and library code were committed in 2fa0a45 and 07c7744; the file's `gitHash`
names the branch tip at build time (d742868, marked dirty).

Elastic induced refraction per unit intensity at the calibrated stiffness (scale 1), D:

| Group | ΔM | ΔJ0 | ΔJ45 |
|---|---|---|---|
| muscles, distance gaze | +0.137 | −0.064 | −0.016 |
| muscles, near gaze | +0.122 | −0.039 | −0.023 |
| lids, distance gaze | −0.084 | +0.195 | 0.000 |
| lids, near gaze | −0.313 | +0.250 | +0.008 |
| ciliary ring, per D | +0.046 | −0.002 | −0.001 |

As in M4, the lids give with-the-rule and the muscles against-the-rule astigmatism. The
responses scale almost inversely with stiffness (scale 0.5 to 4: lids at distance ΔJ0 +0.313,
+0.195, +0.109, +0.057 D). The growth refractions per unit growth amplitude are large in M (the
homeostatic mode is purely spherical, +88 D per unit, and every group's growth pattern has a
spherical part) and, for the lids, in J0 (+31 to +47 D per unit), because a unit amplitude is a
large growth strain; the calibrated rate sets the amplitudes.

**Growth feedback eigenvalues** (per unit of `r·t`): at scale 1 they run from −58.5 to −0.42; at
scale 2 to −1.9 and at scale 4 to −4.8; at scale 0.5 the least stable one is **+0.31**. The
reduced growth dynamics are therefore stable at and above the calibrated stiffness but have one
mode that is barely restored at the calibrated stiffness and grows at half of it. In-plane growth
of a pressurized shell enlarges and thins it, which raises its membrane stress (Laplace's law),
so a stress-driven in-plane growth law without thickening is close to unstable for its spherical
mode; the coarse-mesh library of the tests (all eigenvalues negative) did not show it. In the
forward model the stiffness relative to the library's unit depends on the fitted age slope (the
age stiffening is clamped below 50 years and anchored at the rigidity calibration's age of 70):
below 50 years it is 1.54 at slope 0, 1.22 at 0.012, 0.85 at 0.03, and 0.5 at 0.056, so steep
slopes reach the growing mode in young eyes, and every slope keeps the spherical mode only weakly
restored. This is why the calibration (Task 5.10) finds large spherical changes wherever growth
is fast enough to move the astigmatism.

### The growth basis against the FEM (M5 review round 1, Task 5.14 Step 4)

**The committed `library-v1.json`, and the posterior and grid built from it, use the load
patterns alone as the growth basis (Krylov depth 0), which does not follow the FEM's growth in
J0.** They are superseded by a rebuild with the enriched basis described here (Task 5.14 Step 5).

The round-1 review's stronger slow test compares growth increments (remodeled minus elastic)
with explicit FEM growth on the coarse test mesh (`n_face 6`, document geometry as stress-free,
calibrated stiffness, half the near-gaze loads, r = 1/yr, steps at r dt λ = 0.45). The span of
the six load patterns restores an astigmatic growth mode that the FEM grows. The library now
adds a Krylov sequence to the basis: the feedback drives of the newest patterns, orthogonalized
and normalized, for `KRYLOV_DEPTH` = 6 rounds (42 patterns). Growth increments, D:

| r·t | FEM ΔM | FEM ΔJ0 | depth 0 ΔM | depth 0 ΔJ0 | depth 6 ΔM | depth 6 ΔJ0 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.002 | +0.048 | −0.024 | +0.047 | −0.007 | +0.048 | −0.024 |
| 0.005 | +0.112 | −0.066 | +0.093 | −0.002 | +0.109 | −0.061 |
| 0.010 | +0.214 | −0.117 | +0.142 | +0.018 | +0.203 | −0.111 |

(FEM at r·t 0.0052 and 0.00995.) Depths 4 and 6 agree within 6% in M and J0 up to r·t = 0.01
at 0.5, 1, and 2 times the calibrated stiffness. Beyond it they stop agreeing (at r·t = 0.05,
depth 4 and 6 give ΔM +0.65 and +0.80 D at scale 1, +0.48 and +0.66 D at scale 2): the slow
growth modes do not converge with the depth. Among them is a spherical mode that **grows** at
the calibrated stiffness. Its eigenvalue on the coarse mesh is +0.53, +0.58, +0.81, +0.95,
+0.95, and +0.99 per unit r·t at depths 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, and 8, against −1.45 at depth 0. It
grows below about twice the calibrated stiffness (+0.86, +0.58, +0.09, and −0.81 at 0.5, 1, 2,
and 4 times the calibrated stiffness, depth 3). The depth-0 basis made this mode look restored.

The FEM itself fails shortly beyond r·t = 0.01. Its ΔM rate more than doubles between r·t 0.010
and 0.012 (+0.047 D per 0.002 of r·t before, +0.106 D after). The largest growth strain sits at
the first growing points outside the clamped optic-nerve cap (2.76 mm from the axis; cap radius
2.55 mm) and grows by 60% over that interval, and before r·t 0.014 an element inverts. The median growth strain stays smooth. The
explicit growth law therefore runs away at the edge of the rigid support, an artifact of the
support model, before the slow modes matter.

So the library records a **validated growth exposure** `validRateTime` = 0.01 (r·t from the
growth start at birth). `Library.simulate` refuses longer exposures, and the calibration caps
the rate at 0.01 / 90 years ≈ 1.1 × 10⁻⁴ per year, against the 0.033–0.079 per year of the
committed posterior. The validation is on the coarse mesh only. The production mesh resolves the
clamp edge more finely, and its runaway may start at a different exposure (follow-up).

## The calibration (M5, Task 5.10)

Least squares from the priors' starting values, then the ensemble sampler from around the
least-squares optimum: 24 walkers, 1 500 steps, 500 discarded, acceptance 0.39, 52 min. The
cohort is 300 synthetic subjects (common random numbers). The fitted targets are the
axis-class proportions among astigmatic eyes of the Zibo City children (6–12 years) and of the
two adult bands of Read, Collins & Carney's review (21–30, 61–80; with-the-rule and
against-the-rule only), and the two mean J0 slopes over 30–80 years; 9 targets, 6 parameters
(3 degrees of freedom), cost 22.3 (sum of squared standardized residuals over 2).

| Parameter | Best | Median | 90% interval |
|---|---|---|---|
| `rate_per_year` | 0.048 | 0.051 | 0.033–0.079 |
| `lid_pressure_scale` | 0.95 | 0.78 | 0.54–1.15 |
| `eom_tone_scale` | 0.47 | 0.34 | 0.13–0.74 |
| `stiffness_age_slope` (per year) | 0.0116 | 0.0108 | 0.0017–0.0238 |
| `baseline_j0_mean` (D) | −0.50 (bound) | −0.47 | −0.50 to −0.37 |
| `baseline_j_sd` (D) | 0.47 | 0.41 | 0.20–0.66 |

Correlations: lid pressure and muscle tone 0.93 (the targets constrain mainly their
difference, as Deviation 8 expected), lid pressure and the baseline spread 0.82, muscle tone and
the spread 0.77, rate and stiffness slope 0.60, rate and muscle tone −0.55.

| Target | Data | Model |
|---|---|---|
| with-the-rule, 6–12 y | 0.63 | 0.68 |
| against-the-rule, 6–12 y | 0.27 | 0.13 |
| oblique, 6–12 y | 0.10 | 0.18 |

**Does the mechanism reproduce the with-the-rule to against-the-rule shift?** In the fitted
targets, largely yes: the with-the-rule share falls from 0.68 to 0.12 and the against-the-rule
share rises from 0.13 to 0.60, and the mean J0 declines at about 80% of the observed rate. In the
model the lids' elastic with-the-rule load supplies the children's with-the-rule astigmatism
(the unloaded baseline J0 goes to its bound, −0.5 D, against the rule), and the decline comes
from growth and from the age stiffening that shrinks the elastic lid response.

**But only with large spherical changes, which the data contradict.** The fitted growth rate
moves the spherical equivalent by several diopters: at the best fit a subject with no near work
changes by −4.4 D by age 20 and −8.2 D by 50 (one with every waking hour at near by −4.4 and
−7.0 D),
and a profile of a 12-year-old with 4 h of near work (`eyebiomech predict --profile
age=12,nearHours=4,sphere=0,cyl=-0.5`) reaches −4.6 D (90% band −8.6 to −1.7) at 50. Adult
population spherical equivalent changes by a fraction of a diopter per decade (the Guangzhou
six-year changes in `data/population/refraction-by-age.json`: +0.24, +0.51, +0.26, and −0.05 D
from baseline ages 35–44 to 65 and over). The spherical equivalent is not a fitted target (adult changes are mostly lenticular,
which the model lacks), but a mechanical change of this size would dominate any lens change. An
exploratory scan with the rate held fixed and the other parameters refitted (least squares, same
targets) shows the trade-off:

| Rate (per year) | Cost | Mean J0 slope (D/yr) | Largest absolute ΔM over 6–90 y, no near work or all-day near work (D) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0.0001 | 216.5 | −0.0012 | 0.3 |
| 0.0003 | 83.0 | −0.0088 | 2.8 |
| 0.001 | 82.8 | −0.0099 | 3.4 |
| 0.003 | 107.5 | −0.0090 | 10.4 |
| 0.01 | 50.6 | −0.0158 | 8.7 |
| 0.048 (best) | 22.3 | −0.0114 | 8.4 |
| 0.2 | 22.4 | −0.0029 | 3.3 |

With growth slow enough to keep the spherical equivalent within a few tenths of a diopter, the
model reproduces almost none of the J0 decline and none of the class shift (the classes stay
near a third each). The cost is not monotone in the rate, and r = 0.2 fits the classes as well as
the best fit with a much smaller J0 slope: the likelihood has several modes, and the sampler,
started at the least-squares optimum, describes only the one around it.

**Answer so far.** In this model, muscle and lid loading can reproduce the population's
with-the-rule to against-the-rule shift only through a growth response whose spherical side
effect is an order of magnitude larger than observed. The hypothesis is therefore not supported
in its current form. The weakly restored spherical mode of the in-plane growth law (the response
library above) is the proximate cause, so the conclusion is conditional on that law; a law with
thickening, or the spherical equivalent as a constraint, is the next test (follow-ups). Every
input is unverified or a stand-in (SyntEyes baseline, population tables from excerpts, the
acuity metric), and the induced astigmatism carries about ±15% of mesh uncertainty.

## The prediction grid (M5, Task 5.11)

200 posterior draws, 416 kB, 22 s. It inherits the calibration's spherical drift: a child
emmetropic at 6 without cylinder reaches a median of −3.0 D sphere by 20 and about −6.9 D in
the late fifties (no near work; −2.7 and −6.2 D with 8 h a day), so every subject of the grid
crosses the refraction onset (|sphere| > 0.5 D or cylinder ≥ 0.75 D) between 6 and 7 years,
and the acuity onset (worse than 20/40 on the stand-in metric) by 9. The cylinder of that child
first grows with the rule (−1.3 D at 12 without near work, −2.1 D with 8 h), then shrinks to
about −0.4 D by 50; without near work its axis turns through oblique (148° at 54) into the
against-the-rule range (within 30° of 90°) from 64, with about −0.4 D, while with any near work
it stays with the rule. Only the grid's subjects without near work and without baseline cylinder
reach the against-the-rule range: the subjects start from their observed refraction at 6, so
they do not carry the cohort's intrinsic against-the-rule baseline that the calibration fitted,
and a baseline with-the-rule cylinder keeps the axis. These are the outputs of a model whose calibration the section
above finds inconsistent with spherical refraction data; the viewer shows them as a hypothesis
test, labeled "remodeled", with bands that are parameter uncertainty only.

## The M4 elastic FEM result (2026-10-28, replaced)

Mesh `n_face = 16`, `layers = 1` (55 314 degrees of freedom), two load steps for the IOP-only
reference and two for the other loads from it; about 4.5 min on the cloud session's 4 cores.
Refining from `n_face = 12` to 16 changes the anterior c(2,2) from −0.289 to −0.313 µm (8%) and
ΔC from −0.038 to −0.039 D (4%); `n_face = 8` is not converged (ΔC −0.007 D before the fixes
below). This refinement was repeated after the driver review of b6f5a0d; the lids-alone and
muscles-alone split below was not.

Regenerated after the review of edd021b, which found that the cornea's diagonal fiber families
followed the meridians instead of sitting at ±45° to nasal-temporal/superior-inferior, and that
the height change dropped the second-order effect of the anterior segment's ~117 µm lateral
shift. Anterior height change (µm, normalized to 4 mm): c(2,−2) +0.025, c(2,0) +0.224,
c(2,2) −0.269 (before the fixes −0.089, +0.074, −0.267). Traced against the IOP-only state:
ΔM +0.11 D, ΔJ0 +0.018 D, ΔJ45 +0.003 D (ΔS +0.13, ΔC −0.04 × 4); before the fixes ΔJ0 was
+0.05 D and ΔJ45 −0.04 D, so most of the earlier oblique astigmatism came from the misplaced
fibers.

Regenerated again after the driver review of b6f5a0d, which found that the "exact" lateral-shift
term above was itself an artifact: the anterior segment pivots about the optic-nerve support, so
the scleral-spur ring (and with it the lens) moves about 113 µm nasally with the cornea, but the
exported lens stayed on the axis, so the cornea came out decentered against it (29.6 µm of
Z(1,1)). The exporter now removes the spur ring's rigid lateral shift and tilt (a least-squares
rigid fit, axial translation and torsion kept) before fitting the corneal maps and taking the
axial shifts, so the document is expressed relative to the lens. Anterior height change (µm,
normalized to 4 mm): Z(1,±1) −0.06/−0.04 (was +0.06/+29.6), c(2,−2) +0.022, c(2,0) +0.177,
c(2,2) −0.313. Traced against the IOP-only state: ΔM +0.11 D, ΔJ0 +0.020 D, ΔJ45 +0.002 D
(ΔS +0.13, ΔC −0.04 × 4), so the removed decentration had shifted J0 by about −0.001 D. The
heat map (sidecar) still shows the raw displacement, pivot included. The cornea and the lens
move back 42.5 and 42.9 µm relative to the retina, a 42 µm
shorter eye: in this model the muscles' pull, taken by the orbit behind the globe, squeezes the
globe axially. Before the fixes, the lids alone gave about +0.11 D of J0 and the muscles alone
about −0.08 D (paraxial estimates from the anterior surface). Everything here is elastic,
uncalibrated, and built on unverified data (see `data/materials/README.md`): it shows what the model does, not what eyes do.

**Sensitivity to the muscle torque balance.** The length-proxy forces do not balance torque, and
the CLI changes them by the smallest weighted amount that does (plan Deviation 15). In primary
gaze that moves the lateral rectus from 0.116 to 0.182 N and the medial rectus from 0.147 to
0.078 N (the other four by under 3%); each insertion in the sidecar's loads records both
`tableForceN` and `forceN`. Leaving the table forces unbalanced and letting the orbital
reaction's rotational term carry their moment instead (same optic-nerve reaction, 0.40 N) gives,
at `n_face = 12`, anterior c(2,2) −1.03 µm instead of −0.25 µm and ΔJ0 +0.089 D, ΔC −0.18 D
instead of +0.018 D and −0.036 D. The size of the induced astigmatism therefore depends about
fivefold on an equilibrium choice that neither variant settles; Milestone 5 replaces both with
passive orbital torques or the v2 static equilibrium. Eccentric gazes are rejected until then:
the balanced set unloads the agonist there.
