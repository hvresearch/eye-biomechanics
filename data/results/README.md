### Static equilibrium with the passive orbit (M5, Task 5.2)

The FEM no longer uses the table forces as they are, nor M4's zero-moment balance: the six
forces are changed by the smallest weighted amount that cancels the passive orbital moment
`−Kθ` of `data/eom/passive-orbit.json` (about 0.118 N·mm per degree horizontally, Collins 1981
by excerpt), each between zero and its capacity, without M4's constraint that the total tension
stay fixed (`eyebiomech/forces/equilibrium.py` explains why that constraint unloaded the
agonist). Every gaze of the ±30° lattice then keeps every muscle the table loads. Forces in N
(lateral, medial, superior, inferior rectus, superior, inferior oblique):

| Gaze | Passive moment (N·mm, globe frame) | Table (length proxy) | Equilibrium |
|---|---|---|---|
| primary (0, 0) | 0 | 0.116, 0.147, 0.168, 0.085, 0.116, 0.116 | 0.160, 0.076, 0.100, 0.092, 0.106, 0.109 |
| near (−5.47, −19.92) | (2.347, −0.618, 0) | 0.075, 0.199, 0, 0.192, 0.269, 0 | 0.086, 0.095, 0, 0.140, 0.088, 0.001 |
| 30° abduction | (0, 3.53, 0) | 0.367, 0, 0.171, 0.083, 0.149, 0.083 | 0.281, 0.001, 0.096, 0.100, 0.096, 0.098 |
| 30° adduction | (0, −3.53, 0) | 0, 0.464, 0.157, 0.091, 0.080, 0.152 | 0.002, 0.141, 0.112, 0.095, 0.089, 0.102 |

The change is large even in primary gaze (lateral rectus +38%, medial −48%, superior −40%): the
length proxy with the tabled capacities is far from torque balance, and the proxy's agonist
moment at 30° is about twice the passive moment. The muscle load magnitudes are therefore weakly
constrained by this model, which matters for any conclusion about the size of muscle-induced
astigmatism (M4 Deviation 15 found a fivefold sensitivity to the balancing choice).

Forces are `activation × maxForceN` with the length-proxy activation (an approximation, spec
§4.4 v1) and the force capacities of `data/eom/forces.json` (partly assumed). At the lattice
corners the antagonists relax to zero force.

## The regenerated elastic FEM result (M5, 2026-10-29)

Regenerated in M5 Task 5.5 with every M5 change to the FEM: the Navarro 1985 preset with its
lens accommodation model as the input (`data/fixtures/navarro-1985-preset.eye.json`, the eye of
the response library), the stress-free geometry by inverse solve (Task 5.3), the stiffness
calibrated to the in-vivo ocular rigidity (about 3.6 times softer than M4's at the calibration
age, Task 5.4), the static equilibrium with the passive orbit (Task 5.2), the lid bands at the
lid margins, and the non-IOP loads as a linear perturbation of the pressurized eye (Deviation
11). The exporter now places the retina at the free ring at the edge of the optic-nerve support
(the clamped node stays put by construction; its shift is kept as `retinaClampMm`), writes the
heat-map field relative to the lens, and fits the scleral height change.

Mesh `n_face = 16`, `layers = 1` (55 314 degrees of freedom). Anterior height change (µm,
normalized to 4 mm): c(2,−2) −0.200, c(2,0) +0.946, c(2,2) −1.060. Shifts: anterior apex
+62.3 µm, lens +63.4 µm, retina −0.2 µm (clamp node +0.1 µm), so the cornea and lens move
forward together relative to the retina. Traced against the IOP-only state by the paraxial
method (`eyebiomech.optics.paraxial`): ΔM +0.063 D, ΔJ0 +0.102 D, ΔJ45 −0.017 D. The
with-the-rule J0 is larger than M4's (+0.020 D), mostly because the calibrated tissue is softer
and the equilibrium forces differ; in M4 the lids gave with-the-rule and the muscles
against-the-rule astigmatism, and the response library (Task 5.8) splits the groups again.

The induced astigmatism is not mesh-converged: ΔJ0 moves by −16% and then +29% between the
three in-plane refinements (a second layer through the thickness changes it by only 2.5%, so
the dependence is in-plane), so the plan's criterion (the coarsest mesh whose ΔJ0 is within 5% of

quadrature points inside it, without the resultant normalization the muscle contact bands
have), so it does not explain the swings on its own; the corneal fit within 4 mm samples few
nodes at these meshes and is a second candidate. The induced J0 at any of these meshes carries an
uncertainty of about ±15% from discretization alone, which the calibration's load scales absorb
in magnitude but which remains in the split between groups. Resolving it (a band integrated
exactly, a locally refined cornea) is a follow-up.

## The response library (M5, Task 5.8)

Built at `n_face = 20`, one layer (the mesh study above). The first build (Task 5.8, 10 681 s)
used the load patterns alone as the growth basis. The committed file is the rebuild with the
Krylov-enriched basis (Task 5.14 Step 5, 4 810 s, from 25b9160; see "The growth basis against
the FEM" below). The elastic responses are the same in both builds.

Elastic induced refraction per unit intensity at the calibrated stiffness (scale 1), D:

| Group | ΔM | ΔJ0 | ΔJ45 |
|---|---|---|---|
| muscles, distance gaze | +0.137 | −0.064 | −0.016 |
| muscles, near gaze | +0.122 | −0.039 | −0.023 |
| lids, distance gaze | −0.084 | +0.195 | 0.000 |
| lids, near gaze | −0.313 | +0.250 | +0.008 |
| ciliary ring, per D | +0.046 | −0.002 | −0.001 |

As in M4, the lids give with-the-rule and the muscles against-the-rule astigmatism. The
responses scale almost inversely with stiffness (scale 0.5 to 4: lids at distance ΔJ0 +0.313,
+0.195, +0.109, +0.057 D). The growth refractions per unit growth amplitude are large in M (the
homeostatic mode is purely spherical, +88 D per unit, and every group's growth pattern has a
spherical part) and, for the lids, in J0 (+31 to +47 D per unit), because a unit amplitude is a
large growth strain; the calibrated rate sets the amplitudes.

**Growth feedback eigenvalues** (per unit of `r·t`, Krylov-enriched basis): the least stable
one is **positive at every tabled stiffness**, +1.06, +1.01, +0.94, and +0.84 at 0.5, 1, 2, and
4 times the calibrated stiffness, and its eigenvector's refraction is almost purely spherical.
The next ones are −1.3, −2.4, −3.2, and −3.9, and the fastest −1 100 to −8 900. In-plane growth
of a pressurized shell enlarges and thins it, which raises its membrane stress (Laplace's law),
so a stress-driven in-plane growth law without thickening is unstable in its spherical mode. The
first build's basis (the load patterns alone) made this mode look restored, −0.42 at scale 1
(+0.31 at scale 0.5), and its calibration found large spherical changes wherever growth was fast
enough to move the astigmatism. At the growth exposures the library is validated for (r·t ≤
0.01), the unstable mode grows by at most about 1%.

### The growth basis against the FEM (M5 review round 1, Task 5.14 Step 4)

The first library, and the first posterior and grid built from it, used the load patterns
alone as the growth basis (Krylov depth 0), which does not follow the FEM's growth in J0. The
committed library, posterior, and grid are rebuilt with the enriched basis described here (Task
5.14 Step 5).

The round-1 review's stronger slow test compares growth increments (remodeled minus elastic)
with explicit FEM growth on the coarse test mesh (`n_face 6`, document geometry as stress-free,
calibrated stiffness, half the near-gaze loads, r = 1/yr, steps at r dt λ = 0.45). The span of
the six load patterns restores an astigmatic growth mode that the FEM grows. The library now
adds a Krylov sequence to the basis: the feedback drives of the newest patterns, orthogonalized
and normalized, for `KRYLOV_DEPTH` = 6 rounds (42 patterns). Growth increments, D:

| r·t | FEM ΔM | FEM ΔJ0 | depth 0 ΔM | depth 0 ΔJ0 | depth 6 ΔM | depth 6 ΔJ0 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.002 | +0.048 | −0.024 | +0.047 | −0.007 | +0.048 | −0.024 |
| 0.005 | +0.112 | −0.066 | +0.093 | −0.002 | +0.109 | −0.061 |
| 0.010 | +0.214 | −0.117 | +0.142 | +0.018 | +0.203 | −0.111 |

(FEM at r·t 0.0052 and 0.00995.) Depths 4 and 6 agree within 6% in M and J0 up to r·t = 0.01
at 0.5, 1, and 2 times the calibrated stiffness. Beyond it they stop agreeing (at r·t = 0.05,
depth 4 and 6 give ΔM +0.65 and +0.80 D at scale 1, +0.48 and +0.66 D at scale 2): the slow
growth modes do not converge with the depth. Among them is a spherical mode that **grows** at
the calibrated stiffness. Its eigenvalue on the coarse mesh is +0.53, +0.58, +0.81, +0.95,
+0.95, and +0.99 per unit r·t at depths 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, and 8, against −1.45 at depth 0. It
grows below about twice the calibrated stiffness (+0.86, +0.58, +0.09, and −0.81 at 0.5, 1, 2,
and 4 times the calibrated stiffness, depth 3). The depth-0 basis made this mode look restored.

The FEM itself fails shortly beyond r·t = 0.01. Its ΔM rate more than doubles between r·t 0.010
and 0.012 (+0.047 D per 0.002 of r·t before, +0.106 D after). The largest growth strain sits at
the first growing points outside the clamped optic-nerve cap (2.76 mm from the axis; cap radius
2.55 mm) and grows by 60% over that interval, and before r·t 0.014 an element inverts. The median growth strain stays smooth. The
explicit growth law therefore runs away at the edge of the rigid support, an artifact of the
support model, before the slow modes matter.

So the library records a **validated growth exposure** `validRateTime` = 0.01 (r·t from the
growth start at birth). `Library.simulate` refuses longer exposures, and the calibration caps
the rate at 0.01 / 90 years ≈ 1.1 × 10⁻⁴ per year, against the 0.033–0.079 per year of the
committed posterior. The validation is on the coarse mesh only. The production mesh resolves the
clamp edge more finely, and its runaway may start at a different exposure (follow-up).

## The calibration (M5, Task 5.10; recalibrated in Task 5.14 Step 5)

Least squares from the priors' starting values, then the ensemble sampler from around the
least-squares optimum: 24 walkers, 1 500 steps, 500 discarded, acceptance 0.41, 67 min, on the
Krylov-enriched library. The cohort is 1 000 synthetic subjects (common random numbers). The
fitted targets are the axis-class proportions among astigmatic eyes of the Zibo City children
(6–12 years) and of the two adult bands of Read, Collins & Carney's review (21–30, 61–80;
with-the-rule and against-the-rule only), and the two mean J0 slopes over 30–80 years. That is
9 targets and 6 parameters (3 degrees of freedom), with a cost of 46.7 (sum of squared
standardized residuals over 2). The growth rate is capped at 1.1 × 10⁻⁴ per year so that the
growth exposure from birth to 90 stays within the library's validated r·t ≤ 0.01 (above, "The
growth basis against the FEM").

|---|---|---|---|
| `rate_per_year` | 1.0 × 10⁻⁵ (lower bound) | 1.2 × 10⁻⁵ | 1.0–1.7 × 10⁻⁵ |
| `lid_pressure_scale` | 2.36 | 2.12 | 1.76–2.42 |
| `eom_tone_scale` | 1.04 | 0.75 | 0.28–1.67 |
| `stiffness_age_slope` (per year) | 0.045 | 0.047 | 0.039–0.057 |
| `baseline_j0_mean` (D) | −0.50 (bound) | −0.48 | −0.50 to −0.41 |
| `baseline_j_sd` (D) | 0.245 | 0.234 | 0.199–0.274 |

Correlations above 0.5 in magnitude: lid pressure with the baseline J0 −0.72, lid pressure with

(Model values with hard classes.)

**Does the mechanism reproduce the with-the-rule to against-the-rule shift?** Only in part,
and **not through remodeling**. The endpoint proportions match: the with-the-rule share falls
from 0.62 to 0.13 and the against-the-rule share rises from 0.14 to 0.60. The mean J0 declines at
about two thirds of the observed rate. The fit is poor in absolute terms, though:
- The target χ² is 86.4 on 3 degrees of freedom (9 targets, 6 parameters). The standard errors
  come from the assumed `effectiveSampleSize` of 200 (`data/calibration/priors.json`), so the
  χ² scales with that assumption, but the pattern of residuals does not.
- The worst residuals are in the children's band: oblique +7.0σ (0.24 against 0.10) and
  against-the-rule −4.3σ (0.14 against 0.27). The 21–30 y band follows at +2.4σ (with-the-rule)
  and −2.6σ (against-the-rule), then the two J0 slopes at +1.5σ and +1.8σ.
- The model's with-the-rule share rises slightly from 6–12 to 21–30 y (0.62 to 0.64) where the
  data fall (0.63 to 0.55). All of the model's age dependence comes from the fitted stiffening
  slope, which acts only above the 50-year clamp, and that clamp is an assumption
  (`cornea-sclera.json` `ageStiffening`: no excerpt covers younger corneas). So the decline
  between childhood and young adulthood cannot be produced.

**The growth rate sits on its prior's floor, and growth there works against the fit** (review
round 3). With the exposure cap the log-uniform prior spans only one decade (10⁻⁵ to
1.1 × 10⁻⁴ per year), so the best fit and the 90% interval (1.0–1.7 × 10⁻⁵) are the floor
itself. At the floor, growth is not neutral. It moves J0 with the rule: switching it off at the
best fit lowers the distance-gaze J0 at 90 from +0.216 to +0.123 D (at most 0.09 D on any
trajectory), about a fifth of the elastic decline from 50 to 90, pushing against the shift. The
sensitivity fit under "Which conditions" lowers only the floor and improves the cost. Within
the validated exposure, then, the data push stress-driven growth as low as the prior allows: a
negative result for the remodeling form at small exposures. The shift comes from the elastic
loads and the age stiffening:
- The lids' elastic with-the-rule load (scaled by 2.4 from the Shaw et al. pressure) holds the
  young eye with the rule, against an unloaded baseline J0 at its bound, −0.5 D against the rule.
- The age stiffening is clamped below 50 years, so the elastic response does not change before
  50 (at the best fit, the elastic J0 of a subject without near work is +0.554 D from 6 to 50;
  with the floor-rate growth it is +0.558 D at 6 and +0.587 D at 50).
- After 50 the fitted slope stiffens the tissue 6-fold by 90 (stiffness 0.63 to 3.8 times the
  library's unit). The elastic lid response shrinks with it (elastic J0 +0.554 D at 50, +0.285 at 70,
  +0.123 at 90 at distance; +0.587, +0.349 and +0.216 with the floor-rate growth), which unmasks
  the baseline.

**Which conditions this answer.**
- The values below depend on the growth rate's floor, which binds. The sensitivity fit with the
  floor lowered to 10⁻⁹ per year (`calibration/posterior-v1-rate-floor.json`: least squares,
  `eyebiomech calibrate --method lsq --rate-floor 1e-9`) sends the rate to the new floor and
  lowers the cost from 46.7 to 44.2. Its parameters: lid pressure 2.72 times the tabled one
  (2.36 at the committed floor), stiffness slope 0.039 per year (0.045; 1.7 instead of 2.3 prior
  SDs above the Elsheikh et al. mean), muscle tone 1.29 (1.04), baseline spread 0.264 D (0.245),
  and the baseline J0 still at −0.5 D. Its worst residuals are the same children's-band ones
  (oblique +6.8σ, against-the-rule −4.6σ). The posterior and the grid keep the committed floor.
- The baseline J0 sits at its bound, so the data want an even more against-the-rule unloaded
  eye than the prior allows.
- The stiffness slope is 2.3 prior SDs above the Elsheikh et al. mean (0.0217 per year).
- The lid pressure is 2.4 times the tabled one, within the methods' 3.8–55 mmHg spread but
  above the between-subject spread.
- At the 90% interval's upper end the slope puts the stiffness below 50 years at the library's
  lowest tabled scale, where `Library._interp` clamps (follow-up).
- The clamp also bites at the top: with the anchor 1.542 at 70, a slope above 0.0477 per year
  puts the stiffness at 90 above the highest tabled scale (4). The posterior median is 0.0472
  and its 95th percentile 0.0566 (stiffness 4.8 at 90, clamped from about 87 years), so roughly
  half the draws are clamped at grid ages 87–90. These ages lie outside every target band
  (follow-up).

The secondary target behaves: the mechanical spherical-equivalent change stays within a few
hundredths of a diopter per six years (the bounding fit below). So the first calibration's
large spherical drift is gone, together with the growth that caused it.

**The first calibration (superseded).** On the load-pattern basis (Krylov depth 0), without the
exposure cap, the fit chose a growth rate of 0.048 per year (cost 22.3). It reproduced the
shift through growth that moved the spherical equivalent by −8 D by 50. That basis misses the
FEM's J0 growth and hides the unstable spherical mode, so the result is withdrawn. The exploratory
fixed-rate scan that went with it (rates 10⁻⁴ to 0.2 per year) used the same basis and is
withdrawn too.

**Answer so far.**
- Within the growth exposures its reduced model is validated for, stress-driven remodeling
  does not help the fit: it moves J0 with the rule, and the data push its rate to the prior's
  floor (removing it lowers the cost from 46.7 to 44.2).
- Elastic lid loading on a cornea and sclera that stiffen with age can reproduce the endpoint
  axis-class proportions and about two thirds of the J0 slope without spherical side effects.
  It does so only with three parameters at or beyond their prior ranges (an against-the-rule
  unloaded eye at its bound, a stiffening slope 2.3 prior SDs high, lid pressure 2.4 times the
  tabled one; 1.7 SDs and 2.7 times without growth) and with a poor fit to the children's band (target χ² 86 on 3 degrees of freedom).
- This is **not a confirmation** of the hypothesis that muscle, tendon, and eyelid loading
  predict the shift. In its elastic form the mechanism is compatible with the endpoints under
  those conditions but misses the childhood-to-adult change. The hypothesis is **untested in its
  remodeling form**. Longer
  growth exposures need a growth law that the FEM can integrate past the clamp-edge runaway, and
  whose spherical mode does not grow (follow-ups).
- Every input is unverified or a stand-in (the SyntEyes baseline, the population tables from
  excerpts, the acuity metric). The induced astigmatism carries about ±15% of mesh uncertainty.

### The bounding fit (Task 5.14 Step 6)

`eyebiomech calibrate --bound-sphere` adds the four Guangzhou six-year spherical-equivalent
changes (+0.24, +0.51, +0.26, −0.05 D from baseline ages 35–44, 45–54, 55–64, 65 and over) as
envelopes. Such a band costs only when the cohort's mechanical change of the mean M exceeds the
observed change in magnitude (SE 0.1 D, an assumption): the lens, which the model lacks, accounts
for the rest. Spec §8's secondary target, mean refraction by age, enters this way rather than as
a target to match. The least-squares fit
(`calibration/posterior-v1-sphere-bound.json`) is the same as without the envelopes: cost 46.7,
the same parameters, and mechanical six-year changes of −0.02, +0.04, +0.04, and +0.02 D, every
one inside its envelope. Within the validated growth range the spherical equivalent does not
constrain the fit. It did constrain the first calibration, whose −8 D drift lay far outside
every envelope. The grid keeps the axis-shift posterior.

## The prediction grid (M5, Task 5.11; regenerated in Task 5.14 Step 5)

200 posterior draws, 395 kB, 28 s.
- **Before 50.** Each subject starts from its observed refraction at 6, and the grid moves it
  by the model's change since then. Little changes before 50 (under 0.1 D: at most 0.071 D in
  sphere and 0.069 D in cylinder): the elastic response is constant there, so this
  change is the floor-rate growth (J0 +0.03 D by 50).
- **After 50.** The stiffening tissue loses the elastic with-the-rule lid load. A child
  emmetropic at 6 without cylinder reaches cylinder −0.19 D at 60 and −0.61 D at 90 (5–95%
  −0.47 to −0.72) with no near work, and −0.41 and −1.00 D with 8 h a day. Its axis is against
  the rule (89°) from 60. The sphere moves hyperopic by +0.33 D by 90 (+0.75 D with 8 h).
- **Onsets.** The refraction onset (cylinder ≥ 0.75 D or |sphere| > 0.5 D) comes only late, at
  84, 75, and 71 years with 4, 6, and 8 h of near work, and not by 90 with less. Subjects whose
  baseline already exceeds a threshold have their onset at 6. 7 of the 90 grid nodes never
  cross the refraction threshold and 15 never cross the acuity one (worse than 20/40 on the
  stand-in metric).
- **The PR's example profile.** `eyebiomech predict --profile
  age=12,nearHours=4,sphere=0,cyl=-0.5` keeps −0.5 D with the rule to 50. It falls through zero cylinder
  around 70 and reaches −0.32 D against the rule (88°) at 90, crossing neither threshold.

## The M4 elastic FEM result (2026-10-28, replaced)

Mesh `n_face = 16`, `layers = 1` (55 314 degrees of freedom), two load steps for the IOP-only
reference and two for the other loads from it; about 4.5 min on the cloud session's 4 cores.
Refining from `n_face = 12` to 16 changes the anterior c(2,2) from −0.289 to −0.313 µm (8%) and
ΔC from −0.038 to −0.039 D (4%); `n_face = 8` is not converged (ΔC −0.007 D before the fixes
below). This refinement was repeated after the driver review of b6f5a0d; the lids-alone and
muscles-alone split below was not.

Regenerated after the review of edd021b, which found that the cornea's diagonal fiber families
followed the meridians instead of sitting at ±45° to nasal-temporal/superior-inferior, and that
the height change dropped the second-order effect of the anterior segment's ~117 µm lateral
shift. Anterior height change (µm, normalized to 4 mm): c(2,−2) +0.025, c(2,0) +0.224,
c(2,2) −0.269 (before the fixes −0.089, +0.074, −0.267). Traced against the IOP-only state:
ΔM +0.11 D, ΔJ0 +0.018 D, ΔJ45 +0.003 D (ΔS +0.13, ΔC −0.04 × 4); before the fixes ΔJ0 was

fibers.

Regenerated again after the driver review of b6f5a0d, which found that the "exact" lateral-shift
term above was itself an artifact: the anterior segment pivots about the optic-nerve support, so
the scleral-spur ring (and with it the lens) moves about 113 µm nasally with the cornea, but the
exported lens stayed on the axis, so the cornea came out decentered against it (29.6 µm of

shorter eye: in this model the muscles' pull, taken by the orbit behind the globe, squeezes the
globe axially. Before the fixes, the lids alone gave about +0.11 D of J0 and the muscles alone

uncalibrated, and built on unverified data (see `data/materials/README.md`): it shows what the model does, not what eyes do.

**Sensitivity to the muscle torque balance.** The length-proxy forces do not balance torque, and
the CLI changes them by the smallest weighted amount that does (plan Deviation 15). In primary
gaze that moves the lateral rectus from 0.116 to 0.182 N and the medial rectus from 0.147 to
0.078 N (the other four by under 3%); each insertion in the sidecar's loads records both
`tableForceN` and `forceN`. Leaving the table forces unbalanced and letting the orbital
reaction's rotational term carry their moment instead (same optic-nerve reaction, 0.40 N) gives,
at `n_face = 12`, anterior c(2,2) −1.03 µm instead of −0.25 µm and ΔJ0 +0.089 D, ΔC −0.18 D
instead of +0.018 D and −0.036 D. The size of the induced astigmatism therefore depends about
fivefold on an equilibrium choice that neither variant settles; Milestone 5 replaces both with
passive orbital torques or the v2 static equilibrium. Eccentric gazes are rejected until then:
the balanced set unloads the agonist there.
